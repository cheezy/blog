Why the Stride Workflow works so well

Most teams start with AI agents the same way: a developer opens a chat window, describes what they want, and watches the agent write code. For one person on one change, that works. For a team it falls apart quickly. Nobody else can see what the agent was asked to do. The requirements live in one developer’s prompt history. Two agents change the same file at the same time. Quality depends on whether the developer remembered to ask for tests. When the work is done, the reviewer has a diff and nothing to judge it against.

Scaling the New Team

In February of 2026 I wrote about The New Team: a very small team built for AI-driven development, with one person who knows what to build and one or two developers whose agents build it. At the time, the post was based on the successes of a few experiments at two different clients.

Since then I’ve used this structure on many teams. It works. The small team consistently delivers far more than the traditional Agile team it replaces, and it does so with less coordination, not more. If your delivery org is currently a single team then this is the right shape for you. So the question I get asked now isn’t “does it work?” It’s “what happens when we need more than one of these teams?”

Send Your Audit Log to Your Security Tools

Audit log streaming and signed exports are part of the Organization plan.

Your security team doesn’t want to log in to every tool your company uses to see what happened in it. They want every tool’s security events in one place: Splunk, Datadog or the system your security operations centre already watches. There they can search them, alert on them and keep them as long as your policies say.

Until now, getting Stride’s events there meant exporting the Organization audit log by hand. Today Stride does it for you. Audit log streaming sends your Organization’s audit log to Splunk, Datadog or any HTTPS endpoint, as it happens. Signed exports let an auditor check that an exported file really came from Stride and hasn’t been changed since.
